The useful first evidence

A commercial electricity bill tells us where to begin.

A recent bill can reveal consumption, tariff structure, supply charges, demand charges and — where solar already exists — exports. More complex sites may also need interval data to show the daily load profile.

Sizing the project

Do not start by filling the roof.

The strongest commercial system size is usually the one that matches the site’s useful energy demand and investment goals — not simply the maximum number of panels that will fit.

Commercial solar is an investment decision.
Operating hours, self-consumption, tariffs and future demand should drive the recommendation.
Daytime use

Solar has the greatest direct value when generation can replace electricity the business would otherwise buy from the grid.

Operating pattern

Weekdays, weekends, seasons and shutdown periods can materially change the useful system size.

Future demand

Expansion, electrification and EV charging may justify planning beyond today’s consumption.

Existing solar and network limits

Older systems, export constraints and connection limits can change the best design path.

Battery decisions

A commercial battery should have a job to do.

Storage may be worth investigating when useful solar is exported during the day and expensive grid electricity is bought later. Certain demand-charge patterns or backup requirements can also create a use case.

The better question is not “Should my business have a battery?”
It is “What problem will the battery solve, how often will it solve it, and is that benefit worth the cost?”
