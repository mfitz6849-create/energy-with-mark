The simplest place to begin

Your electricity bill gives us facts to work from.

A recent bill can show your usage, tariff, charges and — if you already have solar — exports. It does not answer everything, but it usually tells us what to investigate next.

System sizing

Solar and batteries should be sized around your household.

A larger system is not automatically a better system. The useful size depends on what the home consumes now, what may change later and what the project is meant to achieve.

Solar only

Often strongest when the home can use useful daytime solar generation directly or has future electrical loads.

Solar plus battery

May make sense when surplus solar can be stored and used later instead of buying more expensive grid electricity.

Existing system review

Sometimes more panels, an inverter change, a smaller battery or no change is the better next step.

Future energy use

Planning for an EV or more electrification?

An EV, heat-pump hot water, electric heating or other new loads can materially change the right solar and battery approach.

EV charging time matters

Daytime solar charging is very different from evening or overnight charging.

Backup is a separate design question

If backup matters, I need to know what you want to keep running during an outage. That can change battery size and system design.

What the recommendation can lead to

More equipment is not always the answer.

Solar may be enough

When useful daytime generation can replace a meaningful amount of grid electricity.

A battery may help

When stored solar can replace expensive later grid use or meet another clear need.

An upgrade may be better

Existing-system limits can sometimes matter more than simply adding storage.

No change may be right

If the numbers do not support another investment, that should be clear too.
