Usage pattern

Two clubs can need completely different solutions.

One facility may have strong daytime refrigeration or office loads. Another may use relatively little electricity until lights, kitchens, bars and change rooms become active in the evening.

Daytime use can favour direct solar consumption

Energy produced while the facility is operating can replace grid electricity directly.

Evening use changes the storage question

A battery may have a clearer role where daytime solar surplus can replace later grid purchases.

Seasonality matters

Sporting seasons, events and shutdown periods can change annual usage patterns.

Other upgrades can affect sizing

LED lighting, hot water, pumps, EV charging or electrification can change future demand.

Ownership and approvals

Who owns the site should be identified early.

Some organisations own their buildings. Others operate from council, government, school or privately owned property. The project may need approval from the owner or another responsible body before it can move forward.

Do not discover an approval problem after the proposal is prepared.
The site arrangement should be understood early so the project can be designed around the real ownership and approval pathway.
Funding and grants

Start with a useful project, then check current funding.

Government and community funding programs can change. An old announcement should not be treated as guaranteed funding.

Where a current program may apply, eligibility, timing and project requirements should be checked against the current official rules before the organisation relies on it.

Start with the project, not the grant.
Funding can improve an already useful project. It should not be the only reason the project exists.
